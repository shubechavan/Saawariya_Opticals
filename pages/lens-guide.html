The Complete Lens Guide

Everything you need to know about lens types, coatings, and materials — explained simply so you can make the best choice for your eyes.

👁️
Most Common

Single Vision

Corrects one field of vision — either distance or near. This is the standard lens for most prescriptions. Lightweight, affordable, and available in all materials.

Affordable Distance or Near All Ages
Best for: myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism correction
📖
Age 40+

Bifocal

Two distinct zones in one lens — the upper part for distance, lower segment for reading. A visible dividing line separates the two zones.

Distance + Near Visible Line Budget-Friendly
Best for: presbyopia, those who need two separate powers
🔭
Premium

Progressive

Three vision zones blended seamlessly — distance at top, intermediate in the middle, near at the bottom. No visible lines. The most natural correction for presbyopia.

No Visible Line 3 Vision Zones Adjustment Period
Best for: presbyopia — natural, modern replacement for bifocals
💻
Screen Users

Computer / Anti-Fatigue

Optimised for intermediate (arm-length) distances. Includes slight reading assistance at the bottom. Reduces eye strain during long screen sessions.

Reduces Eye Strain Blue Light Ready Work Friendly
Best for: office workers, developers, students on screens 6+ hours

Outdoors

Photochromic (Transition)

Automatically darken in sunlight and clear indoors. Provides built-in UV protection. Eliminates the need to carry separate sunglasses with prescription.

UV Protection Auto-Tinting Slower in Cold

Active Use

Polarised

Eliminates horizontal glare from reflective surfaces (water, roads, snow). Available as prescription sunglasses. Superior visual clarity for outdoor activities.

Glare-Free Sunglass Lenses Driver Friendly
Best for: driving, fishing, beach, snow sports
Coating What it does Recommended? Who needs it most
Anti-Reflective (AR) Eliminates reflections and glare from screens, headlights, and overhead lights. Lenses look almost invisible to others. Essential Everyone — especially screen users and night drivers
UV 400 Protection Blocks 100% of UVA and UVB rays up to 400nm. Prevents cataracts and macular degeneration from UV exposure. Essential Everyone — should be included in every pair
Blue Light Filter Cuts high-energy visible blue light emitted by screens. May reduce digital eye strain and improve sleep quality. Highly Recommended Office workers, students, gamers using screens 4+ hrs/day
Hard Coat (Scratch Resistant) Adds a thin hardening layer that significantly reduces surface scratches from daily use. Recommended Everyone — especially children and active users
Hydrophobic / Water Repellent Causes water droplets to bead and roll off. Makes lenses easier to clean and smudge-resistant. Optional Rainy climates, outdoor users, those who clean glasses frequently
Anti-Fog Prevents condensation when moving between temperature extremes (e.g., air-conditioned room to outdoors). Situational Mask wearers, kitchen workers, cold-climate residents
Mirror Coat Reflective metallic coating applied to sunglasses. Reduces light transmission and adds a fashion-forward look. Aesthetic High-altitude sports, beach goers, fashion-forward sunglass wearers
Photochromic Coat Applied as a surface treatment on certain lenses to add light-adaptive darkening (vs. built-in photochromic material). Optional Those who want transition effect at lower cost than full photochromic lenses

Our recommendation: At minimum, every lens should have AR + UV400 + Hard Coat. If you use screens extensively, add a Blue Light filter. These three together cover 95% of daily eye protection needs and are available across all budget ranges at Saawariya Opticals.

Your prescription slip can look confusing with abbreviations like SPH, CYL, AXIS, and ADD. Here's a simple breakdown of what each term means.

SPH (Sphere)

The main power to correct nearsightedness (−) or farsightedness (+). A −1.00 means mild myopia; −6.00 means high myopia.

CYL (Cylinder)

Corrects astigmatism — irregular curvature in the cornea or lens. Always paired with an AXIS value. Can be + or −.

AXIS

The orientation of the astigmatism correction, from 0° to 180°. Only relevant when CYL is present. Not a power value.

ADD (Addition)

Extra magnification for near vision added to the bottom of bifocal or progressive lenses. Usually between +0.75 and +3.50. Present after age 40.

Good lens care keeps your glasses scratch-free and crystal-clear for years. Follow these tips to get the most from your eyewear.

  • 🧴

    Use a microfibre cloth — always

    Paper towels, tissues, and shirt hems contain fibres that microscopically scratch lens coatings. Keep the microfibre cloth that came with your glasses in your case at all times.

  • 💧

    Rinse before wiping

    Dry-wiping grinds dust particles across the surface. A quick rinse with lukewarm water dislodges particles before you wipe — preventing the majority of scratches.

  • 🚫

    Avoid harsh chemicals

    Ammonia, bleach, acetone, and vinegar strip AR coatings. Use mild dish soap (one drop) or a dedicated lens cleaning spray. Avoid antibacterial soaps with lotions.

  • 🌡️

    Never leave glasses in a hot car

    Temperatures inside a parked car can exceed 70°C. This warps plastic frames, degrades lens coatings, and may separate laminated lens layers. Always take them with you.

  • 📦

    Store in a hard case

    Leaving glasses face-down on a table or loose in a bag is the #1 cause of scratched lenses. A rigid case costs nothing extra with us and saves your lenses.

  • 🔧

    Get regular adjustments — free

    Frames gradually loosen and shift, changing how lenses sit in front of your eyes. Pop into either of our stores anytime for a free frame adjustment and tightening — takes 2 minutes.

  • 🕐

    Check your prescription every year

    Vision changes gradually — you may not notice until you test again. An updated prescription ensures you're not straining with the wrong power. Eye testing is free at Saawariya Opticals.
